This lecture presents a tafsir of Surah al-'Adiyat (100), explaining its opening oath by the war-horses of jihad in Medina, then moving into a wider reflection on the human being's buried fitra (innate nature), the self as witness on the Day of Judgment, and the four worldly attachments — wealth, status, family, and possessions — that can eclipse a person's covenant with God.

Tafsir summary:

Surah al-'Adiyat opens with an oath by the war-horses of the mujahidin in Medina — panting as they charge, striking sparks with their hooves, raiding at dawn and raising dust on the battlefield. The lecture distinguishes this oath from the mention of camels, mules and donkeys elsewhere in the Qur'an (as in Surah al-Nahl), showing that only the war-horse, not the beasts of burden, plays a role in the actual charge of battle. This jihad is what made Islam prevail, and it is the condition under which the promise that the believers are "the best of creation" (khayru-l-bariyya) is realized.

After the oaths, God rebukes the human being — the surah says he is ungrateful to his Lord, a witness against himself, and intensely attached to wealth (understood broadly as property, rank, and status). This censure concerns only the "empty half" of the human being — the clay-born nature — while the other half traces back to God's covenant with humanity at creation, when every soul affirmed "Yes" to the question "Am I not your Lord?" The fitra, this innate divine nature, can be buried and silenced beneath appetite and desire, but it never dies; it remains a living, aware witness within the person, ready to testify against him on the Day of Judgment.

The lecture explains that testimony differs from confession: the human being's own limbs and inner fitra testify against him for the deeds his desirous self committed, in the same way the noble recording angels keep a ledger that misses nothing, small or great. Four worldly attachments — human relationships, precious minerals, livestock, and cultivated land — are what the heedless heart chases instead of the love of God. The path out is to love God above these attachments, following the model of those who gave everything, even their lives, for that love — as demonstrated by the martyrs of Uhud. The lecture closes with a call to examine and revive the buried heart before the Resurrection, when nothing of it will remain hidden.
